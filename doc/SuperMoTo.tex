%-- The hover summaries (plain text only: they become PDF strings) ------------
\gtip{allpass}{All-pass / excess phase: a unit-magnitude factor that changes
  phase only, carrying the phase a response has beyond the minimum-phase
  minimum: pure delay, reflections, out-of-phase summation. Not removable by a
  stable causal filter.}
\gtip{cepstrum}{Cepstrum (real): the inverse Fourier transform of the
  log-magnitude spectrum. Keeping its causal part reconstructs the
  minimum-phase response used for low-latency rendering.}
\gtip{coherence}{Coherence: the fraction of the measured output linearly
  explained by the input at each frequency, from 0 to 1. It says how far a
  Welch estimate can be trusted.}
\gtip{comb}{Comb filtering: regularly spaced peaks and notches produced when
  the direct sound sums with a delayed copy, such as a reflection. Wide-band
  and tied to geometry.}
\gtip{crossover}{Crossover: the frequency region where two drivers, for
  instance a main and a subwoofer, hand over. A flat sum there is a phase and
  timing problem as much as a level one.}
\gtip{fir}{FIR (finite impulse response) filter: convolves the signal with a
  finite list of coefficients, the impulse response. Length N sets the
  frequency resolution fs/N and, if linear-phase, the latency N/2.}
\gtip{groupdelay}{Group delay: minus the derivative of phase with respect to
  angular frequency, that is the delay a response imposes near each frequency.
  Used to auto-detect the main/sub offset.}
\gtip{h1}{H1 estimator: the cross-spectrum over input auto-spectrum ratio
  Sxy/Sxx averaged over Welch segments; the noise-robust transfer function
  SuperMoTo measures.}
\gtip{hilbert}{Hilbert transform: the operator linking log-magnitude and phase
  of a minimum-phase response, which lets phase be recovered from magnitude.}
\gtip{ir}{Impulse response (IR): a system's output to a unit impulse, the
  time-domain twin of the transfer function. Exported as a wav for the FIR
  engine and as the measured-response file.}
\gtip{latencycomp}{Inter-output latency compensation: automatic per-output
  delay so every engaged output stays time-aligned with the longest FIR's bulk
  latency. An output's own manual Delay first self-absorbs its own FIR's
  latency where it has slack. Shown by a gold LED on the output.}
\gtip{linphase}{Linear-phase: a filter whose phase is exactly linear in
  frequency, that is a pure delay across the band. It can impose an arbitrary
  phase correction but adds N/2 samples of latency.}
\gtip{minphase}{Minimum-phase: a causal, stable response whose inverse is also
  causal and stable, with all poles and zeros inside the unit circle. The only
  kind that is fully invertible by a realisable filter.}
\gtip{polarity}{Polarity (Invert): a frequency-independent 180 degree sign
  flip, as opposed to a delay, which is frequency-dependent. Invert flips a
  subwoofer wired out of polarity.}
\gtip{regularization}{Regularization: bounding the inverse where the measured
  magnitude is tiny, so deep notches do not demand infinite boost. Here a
  soft-knee (tanh) ceiling on the correction gain.}
\gtip{roommode}{Room mode: a standing-wave resonance of the room, producing
  large, strongly position-dependent peaks and dips at low frequency, with
  audible ringing.}
\gtip{sbir}{SBIR, speaker-boundary interference response: the cancellation dip
  in the upper bass set by the speaker-to-wall distance.}
\gtip{smoothing}{Smoothing (fractional-octave): averaging the complex response
  over a proportional bandwidth, for instance 1/6 octave, finer in the bass and
  coarser in the treble, so the correction follows trends rather than
  single-seat interference.}
\gtip{spatialavg}{Spatial averaging: averaging the delay-aligned responses of
  several microphone positions, so the correction targets what is consistent
  across the listening area rather than one seat.}
\gtip{tf}{Transfer function: the complex ratio H(f) = Y(f)/X(f) of output to
  input spectrum, magnitude and phase together, that describes a linear
  system.}
\gtip{welch}{Welch method: power and cross-spectra estimated by averaging
  windowed, overlapping FFT segments, trading resolution for variance to give
  a usable in-room transfer function.}
